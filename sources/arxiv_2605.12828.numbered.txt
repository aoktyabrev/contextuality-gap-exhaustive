     8	Quantum contextuality rules out classical descriptions in which measurement outcomes are in-
     9	dependent of the compatible measurements performed alongside them. While the KCBS pentagon
    10	provides the minimal qutrit test, identifying larger but still compact contextuality witnesses remains
    11	important for understanding how contextuality can be organized, amplified, and benchmarked. We
    12	solve the eight-event version of this problem within the graph-theoretic framework for contextuality.
    13	An exhaustive search over all 11,117 connected non-isomorphic graphs with eight vertices identifies
    14	a sparse ten-edge graph, which we call Quad-C 5, as the maximum-gap witness. The graph has
    15	a larger absolute contextuality gap than the Wagner graph, a standard eight-vertex benchmark,
    16	despite having fewer exclusivity relations. Its structure is transparent: it is formed from four over-
    17	lapping KCBS pentagons, with each edge shared by two pentagons. We prove that Quad-C 5 is
    18	already contextual for a single qutrit by giving an explicit three-dimensional construction. In this
    19	qutrit realization its contextuality margin equals that of KCBS, whereas its full quantum advantage
    20	is reached numerically in four dimensions and exceeds that of the Wagner graph. Quad-C 5 there-
    21	fore provides a compact bridge between minimal qutrit contextuality and stronger four-dimensional
    22	contextuality witnesses.
    23	I. INTRODUCTION
    24	Quantum contextuality [1] captures an irreducible ten-
    25	sion between quantum mechanics and classical realism:
    26	measurement outcomes cannot, in general, be assigned
    27	pre-existing values independently of which compatible
    28	measurements are performed alongside them. Unlike
    29	Bell nonlocality, contextuality does not require spatially
    30	separated subsystems and can already appear in a sin-
    31	gle finite-dimensional quantum system. This makes it
    32	both a foundational signature of nonclassicality and a
    33	resource for quantum information processing, with es-
    34	tablished connections to quantum computation, commu-
    35	nication, and sensing [2–4].
    36	Identifying simple yet strong contextuality witnesses
    37	is a central goal in this program. The Klyachko–Can–
    38	Binicio˘ glu–Shumovsky (KCBS) construction demon-
    39	strated that five cyclically compatible measurements on
    40	a single spin-1 system are sufficient for a state-dependent
    41	contextuality test [5, 6]. This pentagonal structure be-
    42	came the canonical minimal qutrit example and was sub-
    43	sequently embedded in the broader family ofn-cycle
    44	contextuality scenarios, where noncontextual inequalities
    45	and their quantum violations admit analytic characteri-
    46	zation [7]. Such closed-form results are rare: separating
    47	classical, quantum, and more general probabilistic corre-
    48	lations is, in general, a hard problem.
    49	Most experimental contextuality tests have focused on

    55	pentagon for qutrits andn-cycle inequalities [7]. These
    56	examples are essential as foundational tests, but re-
    57	cent resource-theoretic developments motivate a broader
    58	question: how can contextuality be organized, amplified,
    59	and benchmarked in larger but still experimentally man-
    60	ageable exclusivity structures? This question is espe-
    61	cially relevant because contextuality has been connected
    62	to advantages in quantum computation [3, 4], commu-
    63	nication [8], and metrology [9]. From this viewpoint,
    64	small-graph optimization plays a role analogous to the
    65	search for compact Bell inequalities or small quantum
    66	error-correcting codes: one seeks the strongest nonclas-
    67	sical signature under limited resources. Here we address
    68	the eight-vertex absolute-gap optimization problem be-
    69	yond the analytically understood cycle family and the
    70	standard Wagner benchmark.

    92	Maximizing the contextuality gap for a fixed vertex
    93	count is a nontrivial combinatorial problem. Adding
    94	edges introduces additional exclusivity constraints that
    95	can suppress the quantum value, yet may also lower
    96	the classical bound and reorganize the geometry of the
    97	witness. Consequently, the densest or most symmetric
    98	graph need not yield the strongest contextuality, and
    99	even the eight-vertex case presents a non-obvious opti-
   100	mization landscape.
   101	Known examples chart the structure of this hierarchy.
   102	The pentagonC 5 realizes the KCBS witness, and the
   103	seven-cycleC 7 belongs to the analytically characterized
   104	n-cycle family [7]. At eight vertices, the Wagner graph
   105	has served as a standard non-cycle benchmark [10, 11].
   106	The full eight-vertex landscape, however, extends well
   107	beyond these symmetric examples, and it is not a pri-
   108	ori clear whether any of these known graphs achieves the
   109	global maximum gap or whether a different, less symmet-
   110	ric structure outperforms them all.
   111	A closely related graph-enumeration program was used
   112	in the study of fully contextual correlations, where the
   113	independence number, Lov´ asz theta number, and frac-
   114	tional packing number were computed for nonisomor-
   115	phic graphs with fewer than 11 vertices [12]. This
   116	database was later used to identify experimentally ac-
   117	cessible graphs maximizing the ratioϑ(G)/α(G), a rel-
   118	ative measure of contextuality strength [13]. The ra-
   119	tioϑ(G)/α(G) has since been studied as a graph-based
   120	dimension witness [31], and the CSW exclusivity-graph
   121	framework has been extended to linear games and Bell
   122	inequalities [30]. Building on this graph-theoretic view-
   123	point, we focus on a complementary optimization cri-
   124	terion: the absolute CSW gap ∆(G) =ϑ(G)−α(G).
   125	This criterion selects compact witnesses with the largest
   126	absolute quantum advantage, determined by the largest
   127	additive separation between the quantum and noncontex-
   128	tual bounds. We use it to identify the optimal connected
   129	eight-vertex graph and then analyze the structure, di-
   130	mension dependence, and noise robustness of the result-
   131	ing witness. Maximizingϑ(G)/α(G) favors large relative
   132	violations, often with smaller classical bounds, whereas
   133	maximizingϑ(G)−α(G) favors large additive separations
   134	between the quantum and noncontextual limits. These
   135	two objectives need not select the same graph, and at
   136	eight vertices they do not.
   137	We resolve this question by an exhaustive semidefinite-
   138	programming search over all connected eight-vertex
   139	graphs. The numerically certified global maximum is at-
   140	tained by a ten-edge graph, which we call Quad-C 5, that
   141	outperforms both the Wagner graph and every cycle in
   142	this vertex range. Its structure is transparent: four over-
   143	lapping KCBS pentagons share edges in a regular pat-
   144	tern, providing a compact mechanism by which KCBS-
   145	type contextuality is efficiently combined within a larger
   146	exclusivity graph.
   147	We additionally determine the minimal Hilbert-space
   148	dimension needed to realize this advantage. An explicit
   149	three-dimensional orthogonal representation establishes
   150	that Quad-C 5 is already qutrit-contextual, and numeri-
   151	cal evidence shows that its full graph-theoretic quantum
   152	value is first reached in four dimensions. The graph thus
   153	bridges minimal qutrit contextuality and the regime of
   154	stronger four-dimensional witnesses; we compare its noise
   155	robustness against both KCBS and Wagner.

   161	and its structural properties. Section V analyzes dimen-

   225	III. METHODS
   226	A. Exhaustive Graph Enumeration
   227	McKay’s database [15] (graph8.g6) contains all 12,346
   228	non-isomorphic simple graphs onn= 8 vertices; of these,
   229	11,117 are connected (OEIS A001349). We retrieved all
   230	graphs, filtered for connectivity, and applied the analysis
   231	pipeline to the connected subset. Non-isomorphic graphs
   232	onn= 5 andn= 7 were obtained from NetworkX’s
   233	Graph Atlas [16].
   234	Although related graph databases exist in the litera-
   235	ture [12, 13, 15], we performed an independent enumer-
   236	ation and SDP calculation in order to optimize ∆(G) =
   237	ϑ(G)−α(G), verify the ranking, and obtain primal–dual
   238	numerical certificates for the leading graphs.
   239	B. Independence Number
   240	For each graphGwe computeα(G) =ω( ¯G) via
   241	maximum-weight clique search on the complement, im-
   242	plemented in NetworkX. Forn≤8 this is exact; we
   243	cross-validated all 11,117 results by independent brute-
   244	force enumeration of all 2 8 = 256 vertex subsets, finding
   245	perfect agreement across every graph.
   246	C. Lov´ asz Theta via SDP
   247	We solve Eq. (3) using CVXPY [17] with the SCS
   248	solver [18] for bulk screening of all 11,117 connected
   249	graphs; SCS provides typical precision of 10 −4–10−6,
   250	sufficient for ranking. The top 50 candidates were re-
   251	solved with the CLARABEL solver (precision∼10 −10–
   252	10−12), whose values are reported in all tables. For every
   253	graph in the top 50, the SCS–CLARABEL discrepancy
   254	is|ϑ SCS−ϑ CLARABEL|<10 −7 and the CLARABEL pri-
   255	mal feasibility residual satisfiesr p <4×10 −10, providing
   256	numerical evidence against artefacts. The bulk scan com-
   257	pleted in under four minutes on a standard laptop CPU.
   258	To test the robustness of the ranking, we recom-
   259	puted the leading candidates with two independent conic
   260	solvers and repeated the high-accuracy calculations five
   261	times. The resulting numerical ranges for the top
   262	three graphs are well separated: the lower endpoint for
   263	Quad-C5 exceeds the upper endpoint for the second-
   264	ranked graph by 2.94×10−2 (Table I; see also Table V in
   265	Appendix B), many orders of magnitude larger than the
   266	reported primal and dual residuals. We therefore regard
   267	the ordering of the leading graphs, and in particular the
   268	uniqueness of Quad-C5 as the eight-vertex maximizer, as
   269	numerically certified at the reported precision. A fully
   270	formal certificate would require exact arithmetic or in-
   271	dependently verifiable interval primal–dual SDP certifi-
   272	cates.

   520	V. DISCUSSION
   521	A. Efficiency of Contextuality Geometry
   522	The main surprise is that Quad-C 5 achieves a larger
   523	contextuality gap thanWwithtwo fewer edges. Adding
   524	an edge to an exclusion graph imposes an additional
   525	orthogonality constraint in Eq. (3), generically reduc-
   526	ingϑ(G) while leavingα(G) unchanged or reducing it.
   527	Naively, fewer edges should correlate with a smaller gap.
   528	6
   529	The fact that the optimal graph is sparser than the pre-
   530	viously leading example points to a more efficient contex-
   531	tuality geometry: the four interlocking KCBS pentagons
   532	in Quad-C 5 encode more quantum advantage per edge
   533	than the symmetric 3-regular structure ofW.
   534	The two-fold edge coverage by the fourC 5 subgraphs
   535	(Table VI in Appendix B) is a candidate structural expla-
   536	nation, though a formal proof of this connection remains
   537	open. This is related to properties of the Lov´ asz theta
   538	under graph combinations [11]: shared edges prevent full
   539	cancellation of the individual contributions, allowing the
   540	fourC 5 copies to reinforce each other.
   541	B. Physical Significance of the Qutrit–Two-Qubit
   542	Distinction
   543	A qutrit witness (d∗ = 3) is testable on a single three-
   544	level system: photon orbital angular momentum [21] or
   545	an atomic Λ-system [22]. Such systems avoid the engi-
   546	neering complexity of entangled bipartite platforms and
   547	are more resilient to certain decoherence channels.
   548	A (numerically indicated) pure two-qubit witness
   549	(d∗ = 4) cannot exploit contextuality in any three-
   550	dimensional Hilbert-space embedding: the orthogonal-
   551	ity constraints for its edge set require a four-dimensional
   552	space. This makes such witnesses more demanding ex-
   553	perimentally but potentially more useful as probes of
   554	composite-system structure. Contextuality of this kind
   555	remains distinct from Bell nonlocality, as no entangle-
   556	ment between the two qubits is required [23, 33].
   557	Remarkably,Wand Rank 5 are non-isomorphic graphs
   558	but share identical numerically indicated dimensional be-
   559	havior (η 3 =α,η 4 > α). This structural coincidence
   560	suggests an underlying algebraic invariant beyondϑand
   561	αthat governs dimensional rigidity.

   678	VI. EXPERIMENTAL FEASIBILITY
   679	From a fundamental standpoint, KCBS is the simplest
   680	qutrit witness, requiring only five events; this also makes
   681	it attractive for experimental tests of foundational con-
   682	textuality [6, 22]. On the other hand, contextuality can
   683	have practical value and hence benefit from scaling. The
   684	Quad-C5 graph addresses how contextuality can be orga-
   685	nized and enhanced in a compact eight-event exclusivity
   686	structure.
   687	A qutrit implementation requires eight rank-one pro-

   707	The qutrit realization is non-injective: two pairs of
   708	nonadjacent vertices are represented by identical projec-

   710	sponds to repeated projector terms appearing in different

   725	VII. CONCLUSION
   726	We have carried out an exhaustive search over all
   727	11,117 connected non-isomorphic simple graphs on eight
   728	vertices and identified Quad-C 5—an 8-vertex, 10-edge
   729	graph containing four interlocking KCBS pentagons—as
   730	the numerically certified unique maximizer of the contex-
   731	tuality gap forn= 8. With ∆ = 0.46784, it surpasses
   732	the Wagner graph (∆≈0.414) by∼13% while using two
   733	fewer edges.
   734	Unlike the Wagner graph, which numerical evidence
   735	indicates requires a four-dimensional Hilbert space,
   736	Quad-C5 is a confirmed qutrit witness: high-precision
   737	numerics indicate thatη 3(Quad-C5) = 1 +
   738	√
   739	5, placing
   740	this value in the same algebraic fieldQ(
   741	√
   742	5) as the KCBS
   743	pentagon. In four dimensions, the full Lov´ asz bound is
   744	saturated with orthogonality error below 10−13.
   745	A depolarizing noise analysis reveals that Quad-C 5 at
   746	d= 3 shares the critical visibilityv ∗ = 1/(3
   747	√
   748	5−5)≈
   749	0.585 of the KCBS witness, an equality that follows an-
   750	alytically from a uniform shift of the graph parameters
   751	α,η 3, andn/d. Any qutrit apparatus sufficient for a
   752	KCBS test can therefore certify Quad-C 5 contextuality
   753	without relaxing noise requirements. Atd= 4, Quad-C 5
   754	also strictly outperforms the Wagner graph in noise ro-
   755	bustness, requiring 2.6% less visibility.
   756	The present optimization is complementary to ear-
   757	lier graph-based searches for fully contextual correla-
   758	tions [12] and for graphs maximizing the relative mea-
   759	sureϑ(G)/α(G) [13]. Those searches identify different
   760	extremal structures. Here the objective is the absolute
   761	CSW gap ∆(G) =ϑ(G)−α(G), for which Quad-C 5 is
   762	the eight-vertex maximizer in our exhaustive search.
   763	These results show that the optimal eight-vertex ex-
   764	clusivity graph is already qutrit-contextual, while its full
   765	Lov´ asz value gives a stronger four-dimensional contextu-
   766	ality benchmark than the Wagner graph. They provide
   767	concrete graph structures suitable for experimental tests
   768	in three-level and four-level quantum systems. The find-
   769	ing that then= 8 maximum requiresfeweredges than
   770	the Wagner graph suggests that denser exclusion graphs
   771	do not always yield stronger contextuality; the relation-
   772	ship between graph sparsity and contextuality strength
   773	warrants systematic future investigation.

  1028	We emphasize that the qutrit construction in Ap-
  1029	pendix A is a valid orthogonal representation of the ex-
  1030	clusivity graph, but it is not injective: two nonadja-
  1031	cent vertex pairs are represented by identical projectors,
  1032	v0 =v 6 andv 2 =v 3. Operationally, this corresponds to
  1033	repeated projector terms appearing in different exclusiv-
  1034	ity contexts. This explains why the qutrit contextuality
  1035	margin remains KCBS-like,η 3−α=
  1036	√
  1037	5−2. The gen-
  1038	uinely larger gap,ϑ−α≃0.46784, is obtained only when
  1039	the full Lov´ asz value is accessed, which our numerical
  1040	optimization realizes in four dimensions. Thus Quad-C 5
  1041	serves as an eight-event graph whose qutrit realization
  1042	retains KCBS-type contextuality, while its full quantum
  1043	realization exceeds the Wagner benchmark.
